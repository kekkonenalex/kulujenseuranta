A. Instrument · a hardware panel

Square corners and hairlines, like a synthesizer's front panel. Filters are one framed strip; the chosen one is lit by inverting it, white on black, so the accent stays for the one action that matters. Status is a small square "LED" and a word. Quick questions are a ruled list, not a cloud of bubbles.

After: Teenage Engineering's product UI (0 px corners, no shadows), Braun's control panels, the rays' own thin vertical lines.

B. Editorial · a newspaper's furniture

Words do the work. Filters are plain words with a rule under the chosen one, like a paper's section navigation. Actions are text: the main one in the accent and underlined, the other quiet. Status is a word in small capitals, coloured only by what it means, the way a markets table prints it. Quick questions read as a short list of questions, underlined in the accent.

After: the Financial Times (one 2 px-cornered button on the whole page, links carry the colour, uppercase labels), newspaper market tables.

C. Grouped · a settings app's calm

One connected control for the filters, the chosen segment raised in a neutral tone (the accent isn't spent on it). Lists sit in quiet grouped panels; actions are words at the end of a row, the main one in the accent. Status is a coloured dot and a plain word. Quick questions are rows you tap, like a menu.

After: Apple's segmented controls and grouped lists (iOS Settings), Things 3's restraint.

My pick

B, Editorial. It removes the most chrome: almost no boxes, the accent only where something can be done, and status coloured by meaning (blue for buy, amber for trim, as the markets colours already are). It also fits the apps' type, which already does the heavy lifting. A suits the rays best but reads colder and shouts a little in uppercase; C is the calmest but the closest to stock iOS.
